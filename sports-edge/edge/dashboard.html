How to read a game

Polymarket is what one share of that team costs right now, including the taker fee; it pays $1 if the team wins. Books is the sportsbook consensus chance of winning with their margin removed. That's the best available estimate of the true probability. Value is Books minus Polymarket cost. A side gets a Value tag when that gap reaches the threshold and there's no QB warning. Elo is our own model, shown for reference only, because it lost to the market in testing.

Read this before building a 7-leg combo

A combo wins only if every leg wins, so the odds multiply. Polymarket has no parlay button, so this prices the combo leg by leg from its live costs. Use it to check whether a DraftKings or FanDuel parlay offer is fair: enter their odds below and compare.

A positive expected return here comes from small per-leg price gaps multiplied together. It assumes the sportsbook probabilities are exactly right and the legs are independent. Even when it's positive, you lose almost every time, and one ticket tells you nothing.

    Betting every disagreement at the closing line

    Simulated trades, no real money

    Every Value flag is bought on paper at the price when it was flagged, from a simulated $1,000 bankroll. Each stake is a quarter of the Kelly amount, capped at 2% of the bankroll. Until kickoff the latest price is recorded each run, and the trade settles from the final score. CLV (closing-line value) is the last price before kickoff minus the entry price. If it stays positive over a few hundred trades, the strategy is buying before the market moves its way. That shows real edge long before wins and losses do.
